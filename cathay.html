Cathay Pacific and the training pathway

Cathay Pacific is the home carrier of Hong Kong, a founding member of the oneworld alliance, and operates both passenger and cargo services out of Hong Kong International Airport. For a cadet that matters less than the structure underneath it: a defined career ladder, a sponsored licence, and a training pathway that ends with an operating seat.

Career ladder

StageTitleWhat it means
1 Cadet Pilot About 80 weeks of sponsored training: ground theory and flying training, ending with a Commercial Pilot Licence.
2 Second Officer Type rating and line training in Hong Kong, then line flying. The first operating role and the start of the licence-and-hours path.
3 First Officer More handling and decision-making responsibility as hours and experience accumulate.
4 Captain Command and final responsibility for the aircraft and the sector.

Training pathway

The airline publishes the total length (about 80 weeks) and the general split — ground theory in Hong Kong and overseas, flying training at international flight training organisations. The phase breakdown below is my own reading of public information; the authoritative version is the training manual you receive after selection.

  1. Ground theory. Air law, meteorology, principles of flight, navigation, human performance and the rest of the ATPL syllabus, in English.
  2. Flying training. Basic handling and solo circuits, then commercial manoeuvres, instrument flying and multi-crew cooperation at the training organisation.
  3. Licence issue. Commercial Pilot Licence with instrument rating and MCC, examined by the authority.
  4. Back in Hong Kong. Type rating in the simulator, then line training and a line check before flying as a Second Officer.

Benefits listed for Second Officers:

  • Competitive remuneration package.
  • Discounted travel for you and eligible dependents.
  • Medical coverage for you and your dependents.
  • Global children's education allowance for eligible officers.
  • Discretionary year-end bonus and profit share.
  • Company contribution to a pension scheme.

These benefits apply once you join as a Second Officer, after successfully completing the Cadet Pilot Programme. Training itself is on cadet terms.

Questions for the recruitment team

  • Which training organisations and countries are used at the moment?
  • What happens if a phase is not passed — retake options, extended training, and the point at which the contract ends?
  • What are the service period and termination clauses attached to the sponsored training?
  • How is the first aircraft type assigned — results, fleet requirements, or preference?
  • What does the roster and overseas-base arrangement look like in practice?

Why this programme

Three reasons, in order. The pathway is defined: cadet to captain is written down, with the assessment points attached. The training is sponsored and structured, so learning to fly and finding a job are not two separate projects. And the base is Hong Kong, which is home — the same city where the licence will be used.
