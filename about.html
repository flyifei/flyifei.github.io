About

Engineer in robotics, computer control and mechatronics — now preparing for the Cathay Pacific Cadet Pilot Programme.

Background

I am an engineer with expertise in robotics, computer control and mechatronics. I hold a robotics degree from the University of Illinois and have also studied at the University of California, Berkeley.

My working life has been spent on systems that have to behave predictably: modelling how a machine moves, designing the control loops that keep it stable, integrating sensors and actuators, and then proving that the whole thing still works when one part of it does not. I have done this work at globally recognised industrial technology companies, including Bosch, where I led multidisciplinary teams and carried several projects from first concept through to delivery.

Leading those projects taught me the part no textbook covers: a design is only as good as the number of specialists who understand why it was chosen. So much of the job became translation — setting a direction people could repeat back to me, agreeing in advance what "finished" meant, keeping a schedule honest when it slipped, and making sure the quiet expert in the room was heard before the decision was taken. Engineering gave me the systems view; those teams gave me the habit of briefing, listening, and deciding out loud.

I am now moving that experience into aviation. I am studying civil aviation operations and aviation theory, working through the ATPL subjects, and flying — in aircraft and in the simulator. Flying is the first discipline I have met where the engineering and the teamwork are assessed together, in real time, with no chance to reopen the decision afterwards. That is exactly why I want it.

What I bring to a flight deck

StrengthWhere it came from
Systems thinking Years of integrating sensors, actuators and control software into machines that must not fail quietly. Reading an aircraft as a system — power, hydraulics, flight controls, automation — is the same discipline.
Procedure discipline Industrial projects only close when the sequence, the checks and the sign-offs are respected. Checklists and standard calls are the same idea applied to a flight deck.
Communication across specialisms Leading teams of mechanical, electrical and software engineers means making one decision legible to everyone. That is the core of crew resource management: brief clearly, confirm understanding, speak up early.
Calm under constraint Delivering complex projects to fixed dates with incomplete information — deciding with what is available, then owning the outcome.

Current focus

  • Theory. Working through the ATPL subjects — meteorology, principles of flight and human performance first, then navigation and performance.
  • Language. Building toward ICAO English Language Proficiency level 4 and above, and the IELTS thresholds the application asks for.
  • Simulator. One drill per session, logged honestly — including the sessions that went badly.
  • Flying. Real flights whenever they can be arranged; everything recorded in the flight log.
