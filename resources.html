Suggested order: meteorology, principles of flight and human performance first — they reinforce each other and make the aircraft easier to understand. Then the calculation-heavy subjects.

Radio telephony and English

Twenty minutes a day, four drills in rotation.

  • Shadowing. Repeat ATC audio half a sentence behind the speaker, to work on rhythm and linking.
  • ATC dictation. Two minutes of tower frequency, written down: callsigns, instructions, runway. Then check.
  • Phraseology. Read Doc 9432 aloud until the standard calls come out without thought.
  • Ninety-second self-recording. Explain one flight in English, listen back, mark where you stalled.

Simulator drills

One drill per session, flown in Microsoft Flight Simulator. A drill is done when it can be completed three times without looking at notes.

DrillWhat to practiseDone when
Traffic patternC172 circuits: power, flap, descent planning, stabilised approach criteria.Three consecutive circuits without a go-around.
Crosswind landings15 kt crosswind, heading method, flare and touchdown control.Landing within the touchdown zone with no lateral drift.
VOR and ILS approachesInstruments only, no moving map: intercept, descent profile, decision height.Stable by 1000 ft without map or autopilot.
Standard proceduresA320neo cold and dark to take-off using the checklist, read-do-verify style.Checklist completed in sequence without backtracking.
Engine failure and go-aroundFailure after V1: maintain heading, positive climb, decision. Go-around from 200 ft.Both flows completed without prompting.
Long-haul flowA350 sector over 10 hours: fuel plan, ETOPS decision point, time zones, fatigue management.Plan matches actual fuel on landing within reserve.
